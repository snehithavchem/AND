Rendering Reality: Research Interests

At the intersection of quantum physics, computational materials science and chemical reactivity, my research seeks to understand how the microscopic properties of matter govern its macroscopic functionality. I employ first-principles electronic structure methods to explore emergent physical phenomena, material properties and elementary chemical processes at the atomic scale.

>> FOCUS: Quantum Materials & Electronic Phenomena
Exploring the electronic and spin-dependent properties of low-dimensional materials. My interests include valley-dependent physics, Berry curvature, spin-orbit coupling and quantum transport, with an emphasis on understanding how symmetry and electronic structure shape emergent phenomena.

>> FOCUS: Electrocatalysis & Surface Chemistry
Investigating fundamental electrochemical processes at material interfaces, particularly hydrogen and oxygen electrocatalysis. My interests centre on surface interactions, adsorption thermodynamics and the relationship between electronic structure and catalytic activity.

>> FOCUS: Plasmonic & Heterogeneous Catalysis
Understanding the interplay between electronic excitations, light-matter interactions and chemical reactivity. Particular interest lies in plasmonic phenomena, non-equilibrium charge carriers and quantum-mechanical descriptions of catalytic interfaces.

Theoretical Framework:
The Kohn-Sham formulation of Density Functional Theory provides the foundation for much of my computational research: $$\left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 +V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf r) \right]\psi_i(\mathbf r) =\varepsilon_i\psi_i(\mathbf r)$$

From electronic structure and quantum transport to reaction energetics, the objective is to establish a microscopic understanding of material behaviour.

>> THEORETICAL & COMPUTATIONAL METHODS
Density Functional Theory (DFT) | Spin-Orbit Coupling (SOC) | Density Functional Perturbation Theory (DFPT) | Time-Dependent DFT | Density Functional Embedding Theory (DFET) | Quantum Mechanics / Molecular Mechanics (QM/MM).
